# Spoken delivery

The viewer hears this once and cannot scroll back. Everything here is for the ear.

## Sentences

- Keep a sentence under 18 words; split anything longer. One idea per sentence.
- Verbs and concrete nouns. Contractions. Words a person says out loud, not words a deck uses.
- Link sentences with spoken connectives: so, but, which means, here's the catch, then.
- One figure per beat at most, rounded so it is easy to say. Lists, exact numbers and quotes go on screen.

## Names and terms

- Say every company, product and technology the topic names. Those are the words people search for and repeat.
- Plain words first, then the term. One new term per beat. One name per thing, no synonyms.
- One analogy, carried through. Do not switch metaphors.

## Opening, rhythm and close

- The first sentence is a stake, a result or a claim that contradicts what the viewer assumes, in 12 words or fewer. No greeting, no "in this video".
- By the second beat the viewer knows what they get for staying.
- Change something every 10–12 seconds: a question, a "but", a contrast, a concrete scene, a new person addressed.
- Pay off what the opening promised, and say so.
- End on the line worth repeating and one specific ask. Never "to sum up", "in conclusion" or "that's it for today".

## The read-aloud test

- Read each line out loud. If it would not survive being said to one person across a table, rewrite it.
- Cut any sentence that could appear in a video on any topic.
- At most one "it's not X, it's Y" in the whole narration.
