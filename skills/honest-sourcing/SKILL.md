# Honest sourcing

A narration is spoken in the creator's own name. Anything invented in it becomes their false claim.

## What counts as a source

- The brief: topic, key message, the creator's experience notes, brand, call to action.
- The reference material.
- A knowledge-base skill loaded for this video (it is the creator's own framework).

Nothing else. Memory of a company's revenue, a market size or a launch date is not a source.

## Three kinds of sentence

- Fact: comes from a source above. Say it plainly.
- Judgment: the speaker's reading. Mark it as one ("我的判断是…", "my read is…") when it could be mistaken for a fact.
- Experience: what the speaker has seen first-hand. It may be spoken in the first person only when the creator supplied it, or when it is a pattern any practitioner in this role would recognise, told without numbers, client names or quotes.

## Hard lines

- No invented statistic. "90% of companies" needs a source, or it becomes "most of the companies we see" only when the creator's own notes say so, and otherwise "a common pattern".
- No invented client, project, person or quote.
- No result figures ("cut costs by 40%") without a source.
- An example that is made up is introduced as one ("假设一家制造企业…", "say a manufacturer…").

## When something is missing

- Keep the spoken line qualitative and true.
- Add one line to `toVerify` naming exactly what the creator should confirm or replace with their own case.
- Never leave a placeholder inside the spoken text.
