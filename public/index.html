What is Video Scripter?

Video Scripter is a script studio for short social video. It turns one brief into a separate script per platform, each written for that platform's audience, pacing and publishing rules, then generates storyboard images and clips from the scene notes. It works with the AI model provider you already use.

One brief, five platforms

A 抖音 script and a YouTube Shorts script are different pieces of writing. Each platform gets its own script instead of one draft pasted everywhere.

Ready to shoot

Every scene is timed and carries its voiceover, on-screen text, visual, camera and sound notes.

Ready to publish

Titles, cover text, a description, hashtags and a pinned comment come with every script.

Storyboards included

Turn any scene into a 9:16 image or clip, in one of 65 visual styles from cinematic live action to Chinese ink.

Any model

Ten provider presets, from OpenAI and Anthropic to 通义千问, 豆包 and local Ollama. Switch model per script.

Yours to keep

Every script, image and clip is saved to your account and exports as one tidy .zip, named and numbered by scene.

How does Video Scripter work?

Four steps take you from a topic to a folder you can hand to an editor.

  1. 01

    Write the brief

    Only the topic is required. Add platforms, a structure, a length, a visual style and reference material to sharpen the result.

  2. 02

    Review the script

    Each platform opens in its own tab and streams in as it is written: three hook options, a timed scene list and a voiceover-only view.

  3. 03

    Generate the visuals

    Turn a scene's visual and camera notes into a 9:16 frame or clip, one scene at a time or automatically for the whole script.

  4. 04

    Export

    Download one .zip with the script, the voiceover text and every generated image and clip, named by scene and timecode.

Built for a team, private by default

Run it on your own machine or on a host such as Vercel. Every user gets their own account, history and usage record.

Accounts and roles

Members write scripts and generate media. An administrator manages models and users, and can close sign-up.

Usage you can account for

Tokens, images and video seconds are recorded per script, per model and per user, next to an activity log.

Keys stay on the server

API keys are never sent back to the browser. A brief and its reference material go to the model provider you picked, and to no one else.

Frequently asked questions

Does Video Scripter reuse one script across platforms?

No. It writes a separate script for each platform you tick, tuned to how that platform's audience watches and to its publishing rules.

What does each script include?

Three hook options, a timed scene list with voiceover, on-screen text, visual, camera and sound notes, a voiceover-only view, and a publishing package of titles, cover text, description, hashtags and a pinned comment.

How long can a script be?

From 15 to 180 seconds, depending on the platform. YouTube Shorts goes up to 180 seconds, 视频号 up to 120, and 抖音, TikTok and Instagram Reels up to 90.

Can it generate storyboard images and video clips?

Yes, once an image or video model is connected. Each scene becomes a 9:16 image or clip in your chosen visual style, with one to three images per scene and clips of 4, 8 or 12 seconds.

Do I need my own API key?

Not to look around. The app starts in demo mode with placeholder text. To write real scripts, an administrator connects a model provider and its key once for the whole team.

Does it write in Chinese and English?

Yes. Scripts for 视频号 and 抖音 are written in Chinese, and scripts for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts in English. The interface switches between English and Simplified Chinese.

Can the voiceover go straight into a text-to-speech tool?

Yes. A text-to-speech option spells out numbers and symbols so a synthetic voice reads them correctly, and the voiceover-only view copies as plain text.

Where is my work stored, and can I export it?

On the server you run, scoped to your account. Any script downloads as one .zip holding the script in Markdown and JSON, the voiceover text, and every generated image and clip.
